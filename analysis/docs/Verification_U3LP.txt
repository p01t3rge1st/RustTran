Selected Verification Analysis

1	Steam Generator Tube Failure

A double-ended single tube rupture with loss of offsite power is presented in the Maanshan FSAR Section 15.6.4. The initial break flow rate is about 70 lb/sec equivalent to WTR0 = 114.5 t/hr in BasicData table.  This value exceeds the maximum charging pump capacity (CHG = 45 t/hr), thus the reactor pressure and level decrease and the reactor will be tripped either by low pressure, low pressurizer level or over-temperature delta-T.  Since the FSAR analysis does not have the charging control system in place, we manually disabled the charging pump after the break initiation.  The reactor then tripped on low pressure at about 300 seconds.  It coincides very well with the FSAR.  Then on the assumption of loss of offsite power, we manually trip all reactor coolant pumps, lock closed the turbine bypass valve (based on loss of condenser vacuum for no offsite power), and both side’s SG relief valves (the ones close to the reactor).  The only SG steam release is through the main steam safety valves.  Their set point PSV is set at 81 kg/cm2.  

Following are the PCTRAN/U3LP generated figures.  Each one is for one-to-one comparison with the FSAR Figures 15.6.4A figures 1 to 12).  They are commented below:
 
    1. RC pressure – Reactor trip at low pressure 132 Kg/cm2 time occur almost identical for both analyses – about 300 seconds.  Afterwards 2 HPSI pumps start on low pressure.  PCTRAN does not  re-pressurize much as the FSAR did.
    2. Hot leg and cold leg temperatures: After reactor scram and trip of the RC pumps, the loop flow goes to natural circulation. Both analyses agree well.
    3. Faulted SG pressure – PCTRAN shows the main steam safety valves cycled at its set point. FSAR shows a constant.
    4. Pressurizer water volume or level – PCTRAN shows level goes to zero and does not recover. FSAR recovered.
    5. Feedwater flow to faulted SG – PCTRAN used control logic, FSAR appeared having use step input rather than actual control.
    6. Faulted SG safety valve flow – PCTRAN shows valve cycling, FSAR appeared to have a pressure regulated valve.
    7. SG tube break flow – Both agreed well.
    8. SG liquid mass or level – PCTRAN wide range level in meters correspond to liquid mass. Both analyses agreed very well.
    9. DNBR – Since the initiation of the tube rupture to reactor scram the ratio agree well.  After RCP trip and flow goes to natural circulation, PCTRAN’s correlation is not valid that resulted in a unrealistic decrease.

3.2.21   Small Break LOCA 

As contrast with the large breaks, the blowdown phase of the small break occurs over a longer time period.  Core recovery and long term recirculation then follow a gradual blowdown.   FSAR Section 15.6.5.3 analyzed a spectrum of 3 to 5 inch small breaks.  A small break was simulated by entering 45.6% of Malfunction 2 for a 3-inch (45.6-cm2) break.  The RCS pressure, core water level and PCT are compared with Figs. 15.6-37 to 15.6-39.  Note the 3 charging pumps’ head curve is given by Fig. 15.6-4 and translated into the PCTRAN database Basic Data table WHI and PHI entries with proper unit conversion.  
 
A comparison of the PCTRAN results for RCS pressure to the FSAR Figure 15.6-37 shows a fairly close agreement.  The PCTRAN results shows that the core becomes uncovered between 600 seconds to 1600 seconds.  This closely resembles the FSAR results, ie. Figure 15.6-39 shows the core cladding temperature increases to about 2000F (1100C).  The temperature rapidly decreases after the RCS pressure drops below approximately 600 psig allowing the accumulators to flood the core.
